## 🎧 Added

**Sort a pack of beats.** Every playlist row has a small button right after its number, and each click steps the track's mark on: new → in a track → saved → dropped → new again. A track that went into a song gets a note icon, a saved one a bookmark, a dropped one a cross and the row fades. The click never starts playback; the rest of the row still does. A row's right-click menu sets any mark directly. Marks are separate from the marker — the marker says where to play from, not what became of the track — and are remembered per file.

**Search, filter and progress for the playlist.** Under the playlist header: a search box that narrows the list by name (every word, any order), a filter for all tracks, new ones or one mark, and a counter of how far the sorting has got (`34/97`).

**Dropped tracks can go for good.** One from its row's menu, all of them from the filter menu — after a confirmation that also says how many of them notes still link to, since those links would break. Files go to the trash set in Obsidian's *Files and links* settings, so they can be brought back.

**Keys right in the player.** With the player panel focused — a click anywhere in it — bare keys work: `A` in a track, `S` saved, `D` dropped, `F` new again for the loaded track, `↑` `↓` walk the playlist, `←` `→` seek by the step from the settings. They go by physical key, so any keyboard layout works, a text field keeps its keys, and nothing is caught outside the panel.

**Measure a whole pack in one click.** A freshly added folder arrives without tempo or key. A gauge button under the playlist header shows how many tracks are unmeasured and measures them one at a time, with progress in a notice; click it again to stop after the current track. Also available as a command.

## ⚡ Changed

**Transposition sounds much better.** The plugin's own pitch shifter smeared drums and warbled on held notes. Transposing is now done by [Signalsmith Stretch](https://signalsmith-audio.co.uk/code/stretch/) — chosen over Rubber Band after a listening test on real beats: on a par in quality, at a fraction of the CPU. Back at 0 semitones it steps out of the way entirely, so the sound is exactly as recorded. While a track is transposed the sound runs about 0.12 s behind the playhead; that is the stretcher's own latency. Tempo changes during playback still use the browser's built-in stretch.

**A saved copy in another key or tempo** goes through the same stretcher, speed and key in one pass, and starts exactly where the track does — it sits on a DAW's grid.

**The A-B loop zone is gone.** It was switched off in 1.8.0 after catching stray drags, and stayed off. A saved zone's start was always also the marker, so the marker is what remains of it. The third press of Stop, which deleted the marker and the zone, goes too — the marker has its own button; a second press still rewinds and makes the next play start from the top.

**Moving the tempo window applies at once** instead of on the next start: every measured tempo re-folds into the new range, and a folder sorted by tempo re-sorts.

## 🛟 Fixed

- The waveform in the panel redrew sixty times a second for as long as a track was loaded, music or not. It now animates only while playing.
- A track renamed or deleted while its tempo was being measured no longer leaves a record under a dead path in `data.json`.
- Tapping a playlist row on a phone no longer reads the whole file into memory for a drag that cannot happen there.
- Settings this version does not know are no longer carried along in `data.json` forever.

## 🧹 Under the hood

Cleanups from the community catalog's report: the bundle no longer contains a bare `require("fs")` (it came from essentia's loader), the Electron calls behind the native drag are typed, a deprecated slider tooltip is gone. `main.js` is now built for ES2022 — Signalsmith runs the source of its own functions in the audio thread, and the helpers older targets need would be left behind. New tests run the analysis worker the way Obsidian runs it and guard the stretcher's build.
